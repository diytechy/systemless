A bounded opt-in12 comparison is reasonable: the observed execution cost and modest guest-clock deficit support testing whether less instruction work per frontend tick improves clock delivery. They do not establish that instruction-budget pressure is the cause; scheduling, presentation and unrelated host load can also contribute. The deficit is small enough that noise and load matter. Compare all three prespecified paired captures and aggregate medians/ranges. Report intervals and visibility/focus validity, actual worker cpuMhz, execution percentiles, backlog/reset counters and synchronized host load. Reload each setting, verify actual cpuMhz, same startup/message-clear readiness, same Classic mode/score0 and idle input. Maintain save state and origin without reset/import. Enemy positions may diverge even with identical entry timing; label this as an idle-start scene comparison rather than pixel-identical workload. If backlog counters or execution cost do not improve consistently, do not infer a pacing benefit from guestHz alone.

Before considering adoption, original/reference-paced checks are required: up/down travel distance over equal real-time holds, projectile travel/fire cadence, enemy motion/spawn timing, collision/score/life transitions, early and busy scenes, Classic and any separately claimed Delirium mode, pause/resume and menu transitions, and held/released input including browser focus changes. Check calibration changes after cold start, music/sound rate and sync, save persistence/reload plus exported-state roundtrip, and physical controller only if claiming hardware support. Our native pause/input evidence establishes basic behavior at its own baseline, not12MHz fidelity or wall-time parity. Unavailable original, audio or save checks remain explicit qualification gaps; an opt-in result may be retained as experimental without promotion.

Recommendation: proceed with the bounded opt-in experiment, no runtime/default changes. Stop adding candidates unless this test supplies a clear, repeatable reason for the next one. A near60 guest clock alone is insufficient for adoption.

Action-to-capture delays were not timestamp-controlled. This prevents identifying a pace regression, unequal entry timing, or another gameplay-state cause. The candidate differs in the scene that determines emulation work; its lower execution time and restored guest clock cannot be interpreted as a matched performance/fidelity win.

Stopping the planned three alternating pairs is appropriate. Do not pool the unmatched candidate into paired statistics, claim a demonstrated gameplay regression, or promote12. Retain it as exploratory evidence motivating a better controlled reproduction. Default25 remains; restore normal route and close emulator before handing workload onward. Three default samples may be summarized as a baseline with their readiness and load limitations, but there is no valid paired candidate comparison.

Next useful experiment, if revisited: timestamp Play and message-clear/capture events, define visible ship/score/mode readiness, capture short startup state checkpoints at fixed elapsed times for25 and12, and establish whether the state divergence repeats with equal inputs and timing. Then obtain a trusted pace reference and the remaining fidelity/audio/save/busy-scene checks. No further budget sweep follows from this unmatched result.
