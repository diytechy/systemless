Comparison diagnostics

Capture from restart resets the scene and timing together. Keep focused with no input for the matched rotating scene. Capture 20 seconds uses the current state.

Simulation: fixed 120 Hz. rAF callbacks, render calls and simulation steps are separate counters. Blur or hidden state invalidates a capture. Memory is measured outside timing capture and never claims physical RAM. Recorded input demo: hold swing for 12 simulation seconds; it is an optional disclosed workload, not an original gameplay trace.
Scope and persistence

Level selection, completions and hand color are saved for ClockWerx on this browser origin.

Movement only. No enemies or invented failure condition. Goal capture finishes the board. Bonus rings become ordinary dots when grabbed by the player; restart restores them. Original save files are not read. Original scoring, music, campaign, original menus and save compatibility are omitted. Effects start off on every visit. Physical controller hardware remains unqualified.

Fidelity limits: rotation follows an approximate observed experimental 6 MHz reference, not independently qualified original timing. Dot collision uses a logical 48-pixel radius; artwork is shorter. Fonts, dot shadows and goal presentation are reconstructed. The hands use symmetric reconstructed curves and approximate gradients. The goal has the four triangular markers visible in the original reference; their six-state size/color shimmer follows the original sprite sequence at an estimated original-clock cadence. Hands rotate continuously around their original pivots; shading and tiny details differ from the bitmap frames. The collage background remains bitmap. Source-derived starburst flourishes circle the player and goal until the first successful player action, then fade; exact raster sparkle texture remains approximate.
