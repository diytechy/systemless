# Some Dots checkpoint art recommendation — future, unaccepted

**Simplify the checkpoint interiors before native level9 review.** The existing circular silhouettes are already clean; the problem is the many irregular blue/white patches inside them. In the supplied contact sheet they read as faceted or mottled balls rather than the source's compact blue orb with coherent cross/band glints. No asset was altered or rendered for this recommendation. Final appearance and anchor acceptance must wait for original level9 captures.

Inspected private `some-dots/vector-candidate/source-vector-contact.png`, the group300 SVG and atlas metadata, and original source images for frames79–89. The SVG uses a fixed circle centered(5,5), radius4.75, then clips multiple fitted curved color-region paths over a gray-to-dark-slate gradient. That body geometry is not wobbling; irregular internal planes create the perceived facets. The large dark neutral areas and disconnected pale patches also weaken the blue-orb identity. Merely smoothing those paths further will retain the wrong visual organization.

Source evidence distinguishes intended changes from raster detail:

- All eleven main descriptors have the same10×10 dimensions, raw offset(11,11), no vertical flip, and identical alpha masks. There is no source silhouette deformation to preserve.
- Highlight/blue-band coverage changes within that fixed body. The brightest white cluster moves and expands from upper-left toward the center around the middle phases, then returns. This is real directional-light/flash variation; do not replace all eleven frames with one static icon or force the lighting itself to be perfectly symmetric.
- Red marking starts in frame88 and grows in89: the source contains two strongly red pixels then four. Preserve that onset and final selected-state distinction, not only a generic red final frame.
- Tiny stepped boundaries, isolated pale cells and notches within a10-pixel source icon are insufficient evidence for irregular large curved facets. Promoting each color island into an independent smooth patch exaggerates raster detail.

Recommended construction: retain one stable circular body with a restrained blue/neutral shaded rim, a small number of centered, balanced cross/band shapes, and one coherent upper-left glint. Express the eleven phases through controlled glint position/extent and band brightness/width, following the existing source sequence. Keep the underlying body and cross geometry intentional and symmetric; allow the source's directional highlight and red-marker progression to remain asymmetric. Avoid changing the silhouette radius, atlas cells, raw anchors, animation frame IDs, lifecycle endpoints, secondary shadow descriptors or collision data.

Rings and green protection markers look plausible in the contact sheet and do not motivate another art pass from this evidence. That is preliminary styling feedback, not native scale, placement, animation or gameplay approval. The checkpoint's secondary shadow and selected red marker still require native comparison. This report is the only written output; source/candidate files remain untouched and the read-only workload is released.
